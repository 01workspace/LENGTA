# LENGTA Collection 01 — AFTER DARK

**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Product architecture proposal — not approved for manufacturing  
**Brand:** LENGTA  
**Tagline:** WEAR YOUR BEAUTY.  
**Brand philosophy:** OWN THE BEAUTY.

## 1. Collection thesis

AFTER DARK establishes LENGTA's signature design language: architectural sensuality, considered structure, tactile contrast and confident silhouettes. It should introduce the house codes without relying on generic lingerie styling.

The collection must balance statement pieces with wearable entry points. Each garment should communicate its construction clearly and be developed for fit, support, comfort, durability and care—not just campaign impact.

## 2. Creative direction

**Mood:** dark, assured, sculptural, intimate, editorial.  
**Primary colours:** Obsidian and Blood Wine.  
**Secondary colours:** Oxblood and Bone.  
**Hardware accent:** Antique Champagne finish, subject to supplier sampling and skin-contact compliance.

### Design signatures
- Clean, architectural seam placement.
- Controlled sheer/opaque contrast.
- Deliberate strap geometry and adjustability.
- Refined lace placement used as a design feature, not visual clutter.
- Considered closure and hardware details.
- Consistent finishing and clean internal construction.

## 4. Proposed colour architecture

- **Obsidian:** core colour across signature styles.
- **Blood Wine:** collection hero colour for selected statement pieces.
- **Oxblood:** tonal alternative for depth and coordinated sets.
- **Bone:** limited light colourway, introduced only where opacity and lining are validated.

Do not launch every style in every colour by default. Select colourways based on fabric availability, opacity, dye consistency, minimum order quantities and commercial costing.

## 5. Material direction — to be sampled and validated

Potential material families:
- Stretch lace with documented composition and recovery.
- Power mesh / stretch mesh with verified opacity and recovery.
- Satin or satin-look woven/knit for nightwear.
- Supportive lining and stabilizing fabrics appropriate to each construction.
- Hardware with verified finish durability and skin-contact suitability.
- Elastics with tested recovery and wash performance.

No fibre composition, origin, sustainability, comfort, support or performance claim may be published until supported by supplier documentation and testing.

## 6. Fit and construction requirements

Each style requires a technical pack before sampling:
- Front, back and side flats.
- Construction callouts and seam/elastic specifications.
- Bill of materials with supplier references.
- Graded measurement chart.
- Base size and fit model specification.
- Tolerance table.
- Closure, strap and adjuster details.
- Stitch and finishing standards.
- Care-label requirements.
- Fit comments and sample revision history.

### Fit validation
Test across the intended size range, not only the base sample size. Review support, coverage, strap placement, pressure points, movement, opacity, closure security, wash performance and recovery. Establish the final size range only after pattern and supplier feasibility review.

## 7. Product page content requirements

Every ecommerce product page must include:
- Accurate front, back and detail photography.
- Product name and style code.
- Verified fibre composition.
- Fit and coverage description.
- Model measurements and worn size, where available and consented.
- Size guide and garment measurement guidance.
- Care instructions.
- Price, availability and delivery/returns information.
- Clear notes for adjustable features and closures.

Avoid unverified claims such as “supportive”, “shaping”, “breathable”, “sustainable” or “premium silk” unless evidence supports the wording.

## 8. Photography shot list

For each approved style:
1. Front full-garment view.
2. Back full-garment view.
3. Three-quarter styled editorial view on a clearly adult model.
4. Construction detail (lace, seam, strap, closure or hardware).
5. Fabric/texture macro.
6. Fit/coverage view that helps a customer make a purchase decision.

Keep campaign imagery and ecommerce product photography distinct. Ecommerce views must prioritize colour accuracy and garment information.

## 9. Commercial inputs required before costing

- Supplier shortlist and capability checks.
- Fabric and trim swatches.
- Minimum order quantities.
- Sample and production lead times.
- Cost breakdown by component and labour.
- Quality-control criteria and defect tolerances.
- Packaging and labeling costs.
- Freight, duties, taxes and payment fees by target market.
- Returns and exchange assumptions.
- Target retail price and gross margin model.

No retail prices or margin targets are assigned in this proposal because supplier quotes and target launch-market economics have not been verified.

## 10. Development gates

1. Approve collection concept and assortment direction.
2. Select 4–6 priority styles for first sampling rather than sampling all styles at once.
3. Source and test material/trim swatches.
4. Create technical packs for selected styles.
5. Obtain prototype samples and conduct fit reviews.
6. Revise patterns and construction.
7. Validate quality, wash, colourfastness and hardware performance as applicable.
8. Confirm costing, supplier terms, quantities and launch market.
9. Approve production samples and final ecommerce content.
10. Only then confirm launch assortment and purchase orders.

## 11. Decisions still open

## 12. Brand guardrails

- Maintain LENGTA's own identity, product language and campaign world.
- Do not copy VYRA, SAXI or ParisShoes designs or creative assets.
- Keep the garment construction and customer fit experience central.
- Use clearly adult models and responsible fashion presentation.
- Treat every product and material statement as unconfirmed until verified.
